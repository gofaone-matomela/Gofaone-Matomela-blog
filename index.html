My Learning Journey So Far

Academic reflection · 15 November 2026

Adjusting to Tertiary Education and Building Good Study Habits

Starting my studies in Business Intelligence and Data Analytics has been an important step in my academic journey. Moving into tertiary education means taking greater responsibility for my learning. I have to manage my time, keep track of different modules and make sure that I understand the work beyond simply attending classes. This has helped me recognise that progress depends on consistency and personal effort.

One of the things I am learning is how the different subjects connect. Computer Related Mathematics and Statistics introduces the importance of working with numbers and understanding information. Business Process Analysis helps me think about how organisations carry out their activities, while Fundamentals of Business Intelligence introduces the role of information in supporting decisions. The other modules provide a foundation in computer technology, systems development, and web and multimedia development. Seeing these subjects together is helping me understand the broad nature of the programme.

Time management is an area I want to keep improving. When several tasks and deadlines fall within the same period, it can be difficult to decide what to work on first. I find it useful to identify the most urgent tasks, break larger assignments into smaller steps and set aside time for revision. Keeping a record of deadlines also helps me avoid leaving everything until the last minute.

I am also learning that effective study requires active participation. Reading notes is helpful, but I need to practise concepts, revisit areas I do not understand and ask questions when I need clarification. For technical subjects, regular practice is especially important because understanding often grows through applying what has been taught.

As I continue through my first year, I want to become more confident in explaining what I have learned and applying it to practical questions. I know that developing these abilities will take time. My focus is to remain organised, be open to feedback and make steady progress rather than expect myself to understand everything immediately.

Group Work Experience

Group work reflection · 15 November 2026

Understanding Teamwork Through Tuckman's Stages

Group work is an important part of learning because it requires students to share ideas, communicate and take responsibility for a common task. Reflecting on group work through Tuckman's stages has helped me understand that a group does not become effective immediately. Members need time to understand the task, learn how to work with one another and agree on how the work should be completed.

Forming: At the beginning of a group task, members need to understand the purpose of the assignment and what is expected from them. This stage involves getting acquainted, discussing the task and identifying what needs to be done. Clear instructions and early communication can help everyone start with a shared understanding.

Storming: As people begin sharing ideas, differences in opinion, expectations or working styles can arise. This stage can be challenging, but disagreement does not have to prevent progress. Listening respectfully, explaining ideas clearly and focusing on the task can help a group work through differences.

Norming: A group begins to develop a better way of working when members agree on responsibilities, deadlines and communication. Dividing the work fairly and checking on progress can help prevent confusion. It is also important for members to support one another when someone needs clarification or assistance.

Performing: At this stage, members are able to focus on completing the task while working together. Good communication remains important because everyone needs to understand how their contribution fits into the final work. Each member should complete their responsibilities and raise concerns early if they experience difficulties.

Adjourning: Once the task is completed, the group can reflect on the outcome and the way members worked together. This provides an opportunity to recognise what went well and identify what could be improved in future assignments.

Thinking about these stages has shown me that communication and accountability are central to successful collaboration. I want to contribute by being respectful of other opinions, completing agreed tasks on time and keeping group members informed about my progress. In future group assignments, I will also pay attention to setting clear expectations early and reviewing how the group is working before the deadline approaches.

STUDY SUPPORT

Study Tips and Resources

Plan the week

Write down assignment deadlines, tests and revision tasks. Break large assignments into smaller steps and decide when each step will be completed.

Use focused study sessions

Try a 25-minute focused study period followed by a short break. Repeat the cycle and adjust the timing when a task needs longer concentration.

Review and practise

After each class, review the main ideas, make brief notes and practise questions. Return to difficult topics instead of waiting until assessment time.

Learning resources

Use module notes, lecturer guidance, the college library and recommended learning materials as the main sources for coursework. Check that online resources are relevant to the topic and appropriate for academic use.
